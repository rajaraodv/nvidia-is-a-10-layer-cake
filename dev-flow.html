The NVIDIA Developer Journey

A single picture for what happens to a developer who tries to start with Nemotron today — and what should happen instead. Read left-to-right: entry → journey → outcome.

Read these two diagrams side-by-side. The entry points stay roughly the same — developers still arrive from Google, Hugging Face, GitHub, Cursor, NGC, conferences, build.nvidia.com, Discord. What changes is the middle. Today it's a maze of 19+ portals and silent permission bugs that funnel most developers to drop-off. In the optimized state it's a single front door, a forkable cookbook, a real cert path, and a tier-up pricing ladder — which funnels the same traffic to shipped products and recurring revenue. The percentages below are directional estimates, not committed numbers.

What's actually different in the optimized state

Eight things shift between the two pictures:

  1. One front door at nvidia.dev replaces the 19+ portals.
  2. The build.nvidia.com permission bug is fixed — first contact actually works.
  3. A middle pricing tier ($20/mo Pro, $100/mo Max) lives between Free and NVAIE.
  4. Certifications are surfaced — NCP-AAI is the new "must-have," LinkedIn-displayable badge.
  5. The Nemotron Cookbook exists on GitHub with 30 forkable production-grade agents.
  6. 20 researcher voices are publicly visible — Anthropic-style identifiable individuals.
  7. Three new entry channels appear: Cursor MCP server, researcher voices, the Cookbook itself.
  8. Every page is agent-readable — /llms.txt files, MCP-wrapped docs, structured schema markup.

All eight are documented in detail in /modern-dev-marketing with owner, problem, move, and metric per item.
